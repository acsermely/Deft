## Arguments and behaviour

Use `call(method, args)` from `/_deft/v1/bridge.js`. Basics, errors and events: `skill.read name=js-bridge/basics`.

- live wallpaper (`wallpaper`, SPECIAL, one app at a time; no methods; needs a manifest `"wallpaper": {"page": "./wallpaper.html"}`, a same-origin `.html` path with the same rules as `background.module`): the user grants it from the app's card on the dashboard, and the same sheet opens Android's live wallpaper preview, where they set Deft's wallpaper (home screen, or home and lock screen). From then on the launcher shows your page as the wallpaper, at the wallpaper's size (often wider than the screen, so it can pan), whenever the home or lock screen is visible, also while the app is closed. Draw with canvas, CSS or WebGL; `requestAnimationFrame` runs at most 30 times a second (15 with battery saver on) and not at all while the wallpaper is hidden (`document.hidden` is true then and the page is paused; keep work in `requestAnimationFrame`, not timers). The page gets the bridge with the app's grants but, as in a background run, anything that would show a screen or a prompt fails with `needs_foreground`, and revoking `wallpaper` fails every call. Events, sent only while you listen for them (nothing is queued), at most 30 a second: `wallpaper.touch` `{x, y, action}` (`x`, `y` in CSS pixels of the screen; `action` `down`, `move`, `up`, `cancel`, or `tap` when the home app reports a tap on empty home screen); `wallpaper.offsets` `{xOffset, yOffset}` (0 to 1, the home screen's page position; the current value arrives when you subscribe). Not for URL-installed apps (no served files). No ongoing notification: the wallpaper itself shows that it runs. Revoking the grant or removing the app turns the launcher's wallpaper off and Android shows its default wallpaper; the user picks their own picture again in Android Settings under Wallpaper.
- keyboard (`ime`, SPECIAL, one app at a time; needs a manifest `"keyboard": {"page": "./keyboard/index.html"}`, same path rules as `wallpaper.page`): the user grants it from the app's card on the dashboard; the same sheet opens Android's keyboard settings to turn on Deft's keyboard, then Android's keyboard picker to choose it. From then on the launcher shows your keyboard page as the phone's keyboard in every app, also while your app is closed. The page gets the bridge with the app's grants but, as in a background run, anything that would show a screen or a prompt fails with `needs_foreground`, and revoking `ime` fails every call. Only that page may call `ime.*` (your app's other pages, background runs and wallpaper page get `unsupported`). Methods (each fails with `unsupported` while no text field is open): `ime.commitText` `{text}` (at most 1000 characters) types text at the cursor; `ime.deleteBefore` `{n}` (1 to 1000) deletes characters before the cursor (or the selection); `ime.sendKey` `{key}` with `enter` (runs the field's action, such as Search, or types a new line), `backspace`, `tab`, `left`, `right`, `up`, `down`; `ime.setComposing` `{text}` shows underlined text not yet committed, `ime.finishComposing` commits it; `ime.hide` closes the keyboard; `ime.switchKeyboard` → `{switched}` goes back to the previous keyboard (works any time); `ime.setHeight` `{dp}` (100 to 600, at most 70% of the screen; 260 until you call it) → `{dp}`. Events, only while you listen: `ime.start` `{inputType, action, package}` when a field is focused (`inputType` `text`, `number`, `phone`, `email`, `url` or `multiline`; `action` `go`, `search`, `send`, `next`, `done` or `none`; `package` the Android app the field belongs to), and `ime.finish` `{}` when it is left. You never get the text in the field, around the cursor or the selection. Password fields (including number and visible passwords) never reach your page: Deft shows its own small keyboard there, you get no event, and your `ime.*` typing calls fail until a normal field is focused. Successful typing calls are written to the audit log at most once a minute. Not for URL-installed apps (no served files). No ongoing notification: Android shows the keyboard itself. Revoking the grant or removing the app turns the launcher's keyboard off and Android goes back to another keyboard; the user can also switch keyboards any time, or in Android Settings under Keyboard.
- background (`background`, needs its grant and a manifest `"background": {"module": "./bg.js", "triggers": ["alarms", "sms", "calls", "notify.action", "boot", "network", "charging", "push", "geofence", "sync", "notification", "screening", "periodic:30"]}`; `periodic:N` needs N ≥ 15 minutes): when a declared trigger fires, the launcher loads the module in a hidden page on the app's origin, with the same grants and rules, even when the app is closed. Subscribe to `background.trigger` `{trigger, payload}` and call `background.done` when finished; the page is closed after 30 seconds anyway. One run per app at a time (up to 10 waiting, repeated `periodic` merged), at most 60 runs per hour. Anything that would show a screen or a prompt (confirm-each sends, file and intent pickers, sign-in, biometric keys, settings screens, large exports) fails with `needs_foreground`; methods that need the app on screen fail as usual. Only `background.trigger` arrives in a run; other events wait for the app to open. Every run is in the audit log, and revoking the grant on the dashboard stops runs at once. Triggers and their `payload`: `alarms` (one of the app's alarms fired; same payload as `alarms.fired`), `sms` (the app holds the SMS role and a text or MMS arrived; same payload as `sms.received` / `sms.mmsReceived`), `calls` (the app holds the phone role and a call rings; same payload as `telephony.incoming`), `notify.action` (a button, reply or dismissal on the app's notification that did not open it; same payload as `notify.action`), `boot` (`{}` after the phone starts), `network` (`{connected, metered}`, 30 s after the connection settles and only when it changed), `charging` (`{charging}` on plug and unplug), `periodic` (`{}` about every N minutes; Android may delay it). `push` (`{base64}`, a push message arrived while the app was closed; see `push` below). `geofence` (`{id, entering}`, one of the app's geofences was entered or left; see `location.background` below). `sync` (`{account}`, Android runs a sync for one of the app's accounts; see `accounts.own` below). `notification` (the `notifications.posted` payload: another app posted a notification that is not ongoing while the app was closed; see `notifications.listen` below). `screening` (the `calls.screening` payload: a call to screen came in while the app was closed; answer it with `calls.screenRespond` within 4 seconds; see `calls.screen` below). The UI events still arrive when the app opens. Android can refuse to wake a closed launcher for `periodic` (and rarely others); such a trigger is dropped with the audit outcome `service_blocked`.
- keep running (`foreground`, high; `fgs.start` only while the app is on screen): `start` `{type, title, text?}` keeps this page running after the user leaves the app, with an ongoing notification "<App>: <title>" (tap opens the app, Stop ends it). `type` is `location` (also needs the `location` grant), `microphone` (needs `microphone`), `connectedDevice` (needs a `bluetooth.*`, `usb` or `nfc.*` grant), `mediaPlayback` or `dataSync` (nothing extra). `title` at most 80 characters, `text` at most 200. Calling `start` again replaces the type and text. `update` `{title?, text?}`; `stop`. While it runs, timers, sensors, listening, the torch and the clipboard watch keep going off screen, and the page stays `visible` (so `navigator.geolocation` keeps working). Fails with `os_permission_missing` when Android has not given the launcher the location or microphone permission or notifications are off, and `needs_foreground` from a background run. Event `fgs.stopped` `{reason}`: `user` (Stop in the notification), `revoked` (`foreground` or the type's grant was turned off), `closed` (the app was closed, e.g. swiped from Recents), `timeout` (Android ends `dataSync` after about 6 hours a day). Back at the first page moves the app behind instead of closing it.
- background location (`location.background`, SPECIAL: the user grants it from the app's card on the dashboard, and the same sheet asks Android's "Allow all the time" location; also needs the `location` grant, else `not_granted`): `watchBackground` `{intervalMs, minDistanceM?}` (`intervalMs` 10000 to 3600000, `minDistanceM` 0 to 100000, both numbers) → `{watchId}`; works only while the app's `fgs.start {type: "location"}` runs, else `unsupported` "start fgs type location first"; at most 4 watches (`in_use`). Fixes arrive as `location.fix` `{watchId, lat, lng, accuracy, altitude?, speed?, bearing?, time, provider}` (`provider` `gps` or `network`; network fixes only while GPS is silent; `accuracy` metres or null). `watchStop` `{watchId}`. Every watch ends when the foreground service stops or changes type, or a grant is turned off; start it again after `fgs.start`. `geofenceAdd` `{id, lat, lng, radiusM, expiresMs?}` (`id` 1 to 64 of `A-Z a-z 0-9 . _ -`, same id replaces; `radiusM` 100 to 100000; `expiresMs` 60000 up to a year, else until removed); at most 20 per app (`in_use`); needs Android's precise location and "Allow all the time" (`os_permission_missing` otherwise). Entering or leaving sends `location.geofence` `{id, entering}` (queued while the app is closed) and, with `background` and the manifest trigger `geofence`, a background run with the same payload. Android checks geofences only a few times an hour while the phone is idle, so expect delays of minutes. `geofenceRemove` `{id}`; `geofenceList` → `[{id, lat, lng, radiusM, expiresAt}]` (`expiresAt` epoch ms or null). Geofences survive a reboot and are removed when the grant is turned off or the app is uninstalled. While a watch or a geofence exists, the user sees an ongoing notification "<App> is using: Your location all the time". Uses Android's own location providers, no Google services.
