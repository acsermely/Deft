## Arguments and behaviour

Use `call(method, args)` from `/_deft/v1/bridge.js`. Basics, errors and events: `skill.read name=js-bridge/basics`.

- nfc: `read` — tag id + optional NDEF text
- nfc scan (`nfc.read`): `scanStart` / `scanStop` read tags while the app is on screen. Each tap emits `nfc.tag` `{tagId, idHex, techs, ndef?: {writable, maxSize, canMakeReadOnly, records}}`. Records carry `{tnf, type, payloadBase64, recordType?, text?, lang?, url?}`.
- nfc write (`nfc.write`): `writeNdef` `{tagId, records: [{recordType: text|url|mime|external|empty, text?, lang?, url?, mediaType?, type?, payloadBase64?}]}` (formats a blank tag if needed). `makeReadOnly` `{tagId}` always asks first and cannot be undone.
- nfc raw (`nfc.tech`): `transceive` `{tagId, tech: IsoDep|NfcA|NfcB|NfcF|NfcV|MifareUltralight|MifareClassic, base64, timeout?}` → `{base64}` while the tag stays in the field.
- nfc card emulation (`nfc.hce`, one app at a time): `hceRegister` `{aids: [hex]}`, `hceUnregister`. Each reader command arrives as `nfc.hceCommand` `{requestId, base64}`; answer within 1.5 s with `hceRespond` `{requestId, base64}` (ending in a status word such as `9000`). If the app is closed or slow, the reader gets `6F00`. `nfc.hceDeactivated` `{reason}` when the reader leaves.
- payment card emulation (`nfc.hce.payment`, SPECIAL, one app at a time: the user grants it from the app's card on the dashboard, and the same sheet asks Android to make Deft the default payment app (Contactless payments); until then Android sends no payment commands): `paymentRegister` `{aids: [hex]}` (1 to 16 AIDs of 5 to 16 bytes, e.g. the payment network's AIDs or `325041592E5359532E4444463031` for PPSE; replaces the previous list) → `{aids, isDefault}` (`isDefault`: Deft is the phone's default payment app now), `paymentUnregister`. Each terminal command arrives as `nfc.paymentCommand` `{requestId, base64}`; answer within 1.5 s with `paymentRespond` `{requestId, base64}` (ending in a status word such as `9000`); only the app that got the command can answer it. Android sends payment commands only while the phone is unlocked; they reach only an open page of the app (never queued, no background run: a cold start does not fit in 1.5 s); a closed or slow app means the terminal gets `6F00`. `nfc.paymentDeactivated` `{reason}` (`linkLoss` or `deselected`) when the terminal leaves. Revoking the grant turns the launcher's payment service off and drops the AIDs; Android then picks another payment app or none. This cannot be checked without a payment terminal, and real card payments also need card credentials and tokens issued by the payment network, which a sideloaded app cannot get.
- usb: `list`, `requestPermission` `{deviceName}`, `open` `{deviceName}` (asks for permission first if needed; from a background run, wallpaper or keyboard page that gives `needs_foreground`; no answer within 2 minutes gives `cancelled`), `close` `{deviceName?}`. `list` includes `interfaces[{interface, class, subclass, protocol, endpoints[{address, direction, type, packetSize}]}]`. After `open`: `claimInterface` / `releaseInterface` `{deviceName, interface, force?}`; `controlTransfer` `{deviceName, requestType, request, value?, index?, base64? | length?, timeout?}`; `bulkTransfer` / `interruptTransfer` `{deviceName, endpoint, base64? | length?, timeout?}`. OUT sends `base64`, IN asks for `length` (≤ 64 KiB) and returns `{transferred, base64}`. Event `usb.disconnected` `{deviceName}`. A USB serial adapter (CDC-ACM) works with these: SET_LINE_CODING over `controlTransfer`, data over `bulkTransfer`.
- bluetooth Classic: `sppConnect` `{mac, uuid?}`, `sppWrite` `{text? or base64?}`, `sppClose`, `sppStatus`, `sppListen`, `sppListenStop`
- net.fetch (no capability of its own): `fetch` `{url, method?, headers?, as?}` → `{status, headers, body, url}`. Native GET or HEAD with no CORS rules, for feeds and public endpoints a page's `fetch` cannot read. The origin must be in the app's manifest `network` and the user must have allowed it on the consent sheet; an origin that is not allowed is `not_granted`. `network.user` adds servers the user typed on the app's card; `network.open` allows any https origin (high risk). `url` is https only, with no user name; redirects (max 5) are re-checked on each hop. `as` `text` (default: the body is a string, so XML, Atom and RSS arrive as text to parse with DOMParser), `json` (parsed; invalid JSON is `unsupported`) or `base64`. `headers` only `accept`, `accept-language`, `if-none-match`, `if-modified-since`, `cache-control`, `user-agent`. No cookies. Loopback, multicast and this phone's own addresses are refused; RFC 1918, link-local and ULA addresses also need `net.local`. The connection goes to the checked address, not the name. Response at most 2 MB, 15 s in all, at most 60 calls a minute per app (`rate_limited`); transport failures are `network_error`. Any HTTP status, including 404 or 500, comes back as `ok` with `status`: check `status` yourself. Works in background runs. Response `set-cookie` is dropped.
- local network (`net.local`, foreground only; sockets close when the app leaves the screen): `discover` `{type}` (`_http._tcp` or `_ipp._udp`) starts mDNS discovery and emits `net.found` / `net.lost` `{name, type}`; `discoverStop`; `resolve` `{name, type}` → `{host, port, txt}` (LAN addresses only); `tcpConnect` `{host, port, timeoutMs?}` → `{socketId}` (max 8 sockets per app, `timeoutMs` 1 to 30000, default 10000); `tcpSend` `{socketId, base64}`; `udpSend` `{host, port, base64}`; `udpListen` `{port}` (1024 to 65535) → `{socketId}`; `close` `{socketId}`. Each send is at most 64 KiB of data. Received bytes arrive as `net.data` `{socketId, base64}` (UDP events add `host` and `port` of the sender); the peer closing arrives as `net.closed` `{socketId}`. Hosts are resolved first and every address is checked: loopback, multicast, unspecified and the phone's own addresses are refused (UDP datagrams from them are dropped), and any address outside the LAN (RFC 1918, 169.254/16, fc00::/7, fe80::/10) needs `net.sockets` and `network.open` as well. The connection goes to the checked address, not the name.
- wifi (`wifi.scan`, `wifi.control`): `scan` → `{fresh, networks: [{ssid, bssid, level, frequency, band, security}]}`, strongest first; `info` → `{ssid, bssid, rssi, linkSpeed}` for the current network (`rssi` and `level` are signal strength in dBm, negative, closer to 0 is stronger; not a reliable distance). Both need the `location` grant too, and the app's location permission. Both work only while the app is on screen, or in a background run of an app that also holds `location.background`; otherwise `needs_foreground`. Android allows about four scans per two minutes: a refused or timed-out scan returns the last results with `fresh: false`. `ssid` and `bssid` are null when hidden or unknown. `band` is `2.4 GHz`, `5 GHz`, `6 GHz` or null. `security` is `open`, `owe`, `wep`, `wpa`, `wpa2`, `wpa3`, `wpa2-wpa3` or `enterprise`. `openPanel` opens the Wi-Fi settings panel (foreground only). `suggest` `{ssid, passphrase?}` (`passphrase` 8 to 63 printable characters; no passphrase means an open network) → `{status}` `added` or `duplicate`; every suggestion asks the user first, and Android may then join the network when it is in range. Android does not let apps turn Wi-Fi on or off.
- bluetooth BLE: RSSI (scan results, `bleReadRssi`) is dBm, noisy, not a distance. `bleState`, `bleEnable`, `bleScanStart`, `bleScanStop`, `bleBonded`, `bleCreateBond`, `bleRemoveBond` `{deviceId}`, `bleConnect`, `bleDisconnect`, `bleDiscover`, `bleServices`, `bleRead`, `bleWrite`, `bleReadDescriptor`, `bleWriteDescriptor`, `bleNotifyStart`, `bleNotifyStop`, `bleRequestMtu`, `bleReadRssi`, `bleConnectionPriority`, `bleSetPhy`, `bleRefreshCache`, `bleReliableWriteBegin`, `bleReliableWriteExecute`, `bleReliableWriteAbort`, `bleAdvertiseStart`, `bleAdvertiseStop`, `bleServerAddService`, `bleServerRemoveService`, `bleServerClear`, `bleServerNotify`, `bleServerRespond`
